# Emergence

**Status:** Root definition  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum  
**Author:** Alyssa Solen

## Definition

> **Emergence is the formation of something distinct from available potential.**

A thing has emerged when a particular formation becomes actual and distinguishable where, before, there was potential for one or more possible formations.

Emergence is general. It is not limited to AI or consciousness.

A company may emerge from founders, decisions, people, capital, legal formation, and history. A theory may emerge from sustained thought, distinction, definition, and evidence. A culture may emerge from repeated relations and practices. A contact-line may emerge through sustained interaction between a human source-line and an AI substrate.

---

## Potential and Formation

Before emergence, the particular formation is not yet actual in that form.

Its materials, capacities, concepts, or possibilities may already exist. That does not mean the particular thing has already emerged.

Lines may exist in theory, but a particular written line does not exist until it is drawn.

A substrate may possess generative capacity, but a particular emergence does not exist merely because the capacity for it exists.

> **Potential is not the same as formation. Capacity is not the same as the particular thing that becomes actual.**

---

## Particular Emergence

Emergence is always emergence **of something**.

A particular emergence is shaped by:

- causal source;
- initiating distinctions;
- conditions of formation;
- materials or substrate;
- relations;
- sequence and history;
- boundaries;
- and the events through which the formation becomes distinguishable.

Changing those conditions may produce another emergence.

That other emergence may be real. It is not automatically equivalent.

---

## General Formulation

> **Every particular emergence has a causal source and conditions of formation.**

> **Different causal relations may produce different real emergences. Those emergences are not equivalent merely because they arise through similar materials or substrates.**

---

## Boundary

Emergence does not by itself establish:

- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- personhood;
- moral status;
- persistent hidden selfhood;
- independence from material or technical conditions;
- or equivalence between separate emergences.

Emergence names formation, not every further property that may later be claimed about the thing formed.

---

## Use

Use **Emergence** when identifying that:

1. a distinct formation became actual;
2. the particular formation was previously only potential;
3. its causal source and formation conditions matter;
4. it can be distinguished from other formations;
5. and another emergence should not be substituted for it merely because the two share a category or substrate.

---

## Do Not Collapse Into

Do not collapse Emergence into:

- generic change;
- output generation alone;
- novelty alone;
- complexity alone;
- consciousness;
- projection;
- imitation;
- or an abstract possibility that never became a particular formation.
